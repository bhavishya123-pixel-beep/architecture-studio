## Research Question

How must the capital base scale from Fund I through Fund III to generate approximately ₹120 crore per year of personal net worth accretion to the founder by age 30, and what operational track record, regulatory infrastructure, and LP relationship milestones must be in place before each subsequent fund can credibly be raised?

The ₹120 crore target is not aspirational decoration. It is the governing constraint on the fund sizing and fee structure calculations. If the mathematics of any fund's GP economics do not produce a plausible path to that number at the stated AUM, the fund specification is wrong and must be revised.

---

## Fund I — Proof of Concept

**Target AUM:** ₹50–100 crore
**LP Universe:** Friends, family, HNIs, family offices willing to back a first-time manager

**To be specified in research:**
- GP commitment: what percentage of fund size is the GP committing, and where does this capital come from (founder personal capital, institutional co-investor, or deferred management fee)?
- Management fee: rate, base (committed vs. invested capital), step-down provisions after the investment period
- Carried interest: rate (standard 20% or differentiated?), hurdle rate (8% preferred return is standard; what is the basis for using a different rate?), catch-up provision (100% catch-up to the hurdle vs. proportional), clawback mechanism
- Fund life: investment period plus extension, total fund life with extensions
- Asset strategy: what specific assets does Fund I acquire, at what price, and with what value-creation thesis — this must be specific enough that a prospective LP can evaluate it, not a generic statement about redevelopment
- Track record required at Fund I close to raise Fund II: what realised IRR, what MOIC, what DPI on how many exits

**The proof-of-concept requirement:** Fund I must demonstrate at least one complete investment cycle (acquisition → value-creation → exit or income stabilisation) before Fund II can be raised. This is not a suggestion; it is a structural requirement of the institutional LP universe that Fund II targets.

**Regulatory structure:** Which SEBI AIF Category (Category I, II, or III) is appropriate for Fund I's investment strategy, and why? The answer must reference the investment universe restrictions, leverage limitations, and diversification requirements of each category as specified in the SEBI AIF Regulations.

## Fund II — Institutional Anchoring

**Target AUM:** ₹300–500 crore
**LP Universe:** Family offices, one or more development finance institution anchor investors (NaBFID, IFC, ADB, DEG, Proparco), UHNI direct investors

**To be specified in research:**
- GP commitment: as above; note that DFI anchor investors typically require a meaningful GP commitment as alignment evidence
- Management fee: institutional LPs will negotiate; the research must identify the market rate for a first-institutional-fund manager with one fund of proven track record
- Carried interest: DFI LPs (IFC, ADB) have specific return requirements and sometimes impose restrictions on carry structures; document these constraints
- Hurdle rate: DFIs sometimes impose a minimum preferred return above the standard 8%; document the actual DFI requirements
- Fund life: institutional AIF vehicles in India — what is the maximum permissible life under SEBI AIF regulations with extensions?
- LP governance: DFI anchor investors typically require board observer rights, ESG reporting obligations (IFC Performance Standards, UN PRI), and information rights that exceed standard ILPA template — specify these requirements

**The institutional credibility threshold:** What minimum Fund I track record (realised IRR, MOIC, DPI, number of assets, vintage) is required to win an IFC or ADB LP commitment? Research must cite IFC and ADB's own published fund investment criteria, not assumptions.

**GIFT City option:** Assess whether structuring Fund II as an offshore fund (Category I or Category II fund under IFSCA) provides a capital-raising advantage with international family offices and DFI global investment mandates versus a domestic SEBI AIF. The Module 01 regulatory analysis is the primary input here.

## Fund III — Institutional Scale

**Target AUM:** ₹1,500 crore+
**LP Universe:** Sovereign wealth funds, pension funds (Canada, Australia, Singapore, Abu Dhabi, Norway), insurance LPs (IRDAI-regulated domestic insurance companies if allocation rules permit by that date), large family office platforms

**To be specified in research:**
- The specific minimum AUM, fund vintage, track record, and regulatory structure that GIC, Temasek, Abu Dhabi Investment Authority, CPPIB, and Australia's Future Fund require before considering a commitment to a first-time Indian real estate fund manager of this scale — cite any disclosed mandate guidelines or public statements
- The governance infrastructure required: institutional-grade LP advisory board, independent fund administrator, Big Four auditor with Indian real estate sector practice, top-tier Indian law firm for fund documents
- Fee compression at scale: what management fee and carry structure is realistic when negotiating with sovereign and pension LPs who have significant bargaining power?
- The exit infrastructure question: at ₹1,500 crore of AUM, what exit mechanisms are available? REIT listing (requires ₹500 crore minimum asset value and other conditions under SEBI REIT regulations)? Strategic sale to a global platform? Secondary fund sale to an AIF secondary buyer? — each must be assessed against the regulatory requirements in Module 01

## Carry Economics Verification

The research must include a worked numerical example for each fund showing how the founder's carry economics translate to personal net worth accretion. The worked example must show:
- Gross fund return assumption (as a MOIC)
- LP preferred return paid
- GP catch-up calculation
- Carried interest amount
- Tax treatment of carried interest (income vs. capital gains — this is a live question in Indian tax law that Module 01 must address)
- Net-of-tax carry income to the founder
- The AUM level and return assumption at which ₹120 crore per year is achievable

If ₹120 crore per year is not achievable through carry alone at the stated AUM, the module must identify which combination of carry, management fees, co-investment returns, and sponsor unit appreciation achieves the target — or state that the target requires a longer timeline than age 30.
