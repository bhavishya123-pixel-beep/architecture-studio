# Module 06 — Small Vendor Absorption Playbook

**Status:** `SCAFFOLDED`
**Depends on:** Module 05 (for understanding which segments ENIGMA will operate in and therefore which vendor categories matter)
**Consumed by:** Module 09

## Research Question

How can a consolidating platform roll up fragmented small contractors, interior fit-out firms, and modular furniture manufacturers without destroying the ecosystem — and what are the explicit economics that make consolidation positive-sum rather than extractive?

The question of extractive vs. positive-sum consolidation is not rhetorical. Indian real estate has a long history of large developers using their procurement power to squeeze small contractors below their cost of capital, creating short-term margin expansion and long-term supply-chain collapse. ENIGMA's Passive House and net-zero positioning requires specialist subcontractors who do not yet exist at scale in India. Destroying the ecosystem that is developing these capabilities would be self-defeating.

## Case Studies to Be Analysed

**Asian Paints — Dealer Financing Model**
- How Asian Paints extended working capital credit to its dealer network, making dealers financially dependent on Asian Paints and thereby raising switching costs
- The specific mechanics: credit period, credit limit setting, collateral requirements, and the dealer's effective cost of capital relative to what a bank would charge
- The outcome: dealer loyalty, market share stability, and the degree to which the model benefited dealers financially vs. primarily benefiting Asian Paints
- Applicability to construction contractor finance: what would an analogous model look like for a small Passive House-certified contractor whose working capital needs are project-based rather than inventory-based?

**Pidilite — Vendor Stickiness Through Training**
- How Pidilite created the Fevicol Waterproofing Systems installer network and the Dr. Fixit applicator certification programme
- The specific mechanism: Pidilite trained small contractors in product application, making the contractors' skill set product-specific, which created switching costs without requiring financial dependency
- The outcome: Pidilite's market share in the applicator-driven waterproofing segment vs. competitive segments
- Applicability to Passive House certification: Enigma Certification could create an analogous certified installer network for Passive House components (windows, MVHR units, airtightness membranes)

**Trent — Private-Label Conversion of Small Suppliers**
- How Trent converted small local apparel manufacturers into Westside private-label suppliers, effectively absorbing them into Trent's production system while leaving them legally independent
- The specific economics: what Trent paid vs. prevailing market rates, the quality development investment Trent made in supplier capability, and the exclusivity or volume-commitment provisions that created mutual dependency
- The outcome: Trent's gross margin profile vs. Reliance Retail and DMart, and what fraction of the margin differential is attributable to private-label supplier economics
- Applicability to modular construction: what would it mean for Enigma Realty to convert small modular furniture and fit-out firms into de facto dedicated suppliers?

**Japanese Keiretsu — Long-Duration Supplier Integration**
- The keiretsu model: Toyota's tiered supplier system (Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3 with Toyota at the apex) and the specific characteristics that distinguish it from a Western vendor procurement model
- Key features to analyse: cross-shareholding, long-duration contracts, co-investment in supplier R&D, joint problem-solving teams, transparent cost-sharing, and the obligation of the anchor firm to help suppliers survive downturns
- The outcome: Toyota's manufacturing quality, inventory efficiency, and innovation rate relative to non-keiretsu manufacturers
- The specific failure modes: when does keiretsu become a cartel? When does it become a mechanism for large firms to socialise risk onto small suppliers?
- Applicability to India: the legal constraints on cross-shareholding, the cultural constraints on long-duration contracting, and the specific modifications required

## Deliverable Format

A three-tier vendor integration framework with the following structure:

**Tier 1 — Strategic Partners** (5–10 firms)
- Definition criteria
- Relationship model: exclusivity provisions, minimum volume commitments, co-investment obligations
- Economics for the vendor: premium above market rate, payment terms advantage, access to Enigma's institutional LP relationships for working capital
- Economics for ENIGMA: quality assurance, IP sharing, first-call rights on new capacity

**Tier 2 — Certified Preferred Vendors** (20–50 firms)
- Definition criteria
- Relationship model: certification programme, preferred procurement status, training access
- Economics for the vendor: certification as a marketing asset, faster payment terms than market standard, access to Enigma training at below-market cost
- Economics for ENIGMA: wider supply pool with quality floor, reduced procurement risk

**Tier 3 — Registered Vendors** (open network)
- Definition criteria
- Relationship model: digital procurement platform, transparent bidding, performance rating
- Economics for the vendor: access to a creditworthy buyer, transparent payment terms, performance score visible to future buyers on the platform
- Economics for ENIGMA: market price discipline, competitive tension in Tier 2 (firms aspiring to graduate)

For each tier, the framework must include: the specific mechanism that makes it positive-sum (how the vendor is materially better off inside the system than outside it), and the specific gating condition that would indicate the framework is becoming extractive (what metric, if it deteriorates, signals that ENIGMA is capturing value from vendors rather than creating it with them).

## This module is wrong if: the three-tier vendor integration framework fails to retain at least 70 percent of Tier 1 and Tier 2 vendors through Fund I's complete asset delivery cycle, or if any vendor in any tier demonstrates measurable deterioration in financial health (as measured by working capital days, debt-to-equity ratio, or EBITDA margin) after 24 months inside the framework relative to sector peers outside it.
