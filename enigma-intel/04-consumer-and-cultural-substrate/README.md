## Research Question

Which behavioural and demographic shifts in India are causally — not merely correlationally — driving demand for the real-asset segments enumerated in Module 03, and at what pace is each shift occurring?

The emphasis on causality matters. India is urbanising, ageing, and nuclearising simultaneously, which means almost any positive market projection can be made to look plausible by cherry-picking one of these three trends. This module must specify the mechanism by which each shift translates into demand, not just note the correlation. Where the mechanism is speculative, it must be labelled `[SPECULATION]`.

---

## Analytical Lenses

**Urbanisation**
- The urbanisation curve: India's current urbanisation rate versus China, Thailand, Indonesia, and Mexico at comparable income levels
- Pace of rural-to-urban migration: Periodic Labour Force Survey data on net migration by destination city tier
- The distinction between administrative urbanisation (census definitional changes) and functional urbanisation (actual economic activity migration)
- Which city tiers are absorbing primary migration versus which are absorbing secondary migration from Tier-1 overflow

**Household Formation**
- Household formation rate by metro versus national average
- The specific mechanism: as household size shrinks, the number of households per unit of population rises, and housing demand rises with it independent of population growth
- NFHS-5 and Census 2011 data on average household size by geography and income decile
- Projection: at what household size does India's Tier-1 metro housing demand become purely replacement-and-upgrade rather than new-formation driven?

**Joint-to-Nuclear Family Transition**
- The rate of transition from joint to nuclear households in Tier-1 metros: Mumbai, Delhi-NCR, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune separately
- The implication for senior living: when adult children move out, elderly parents either move into organised senior living or are left isolated — this is the specific causal mechanism behind senior living demand
- Cultural resistance to senior living: document the specific social stigma, its current strength by geography and income decile, and the measurable rate at which it is eroding (if it is)
- The `taboo-erosion` hypothesis must be stated as a hypothesis and tested against any available data (NPS surveys, Antara/Columbia Pacific occupancy ramp curves, wait-list data if accessible)

**Post-COVID Rental and Ownership Psychology**
- The documented shift (if any) in rent-versus-own preference in Indian Tier-1 metros post-2020
- Specific mechanism for co-living demand: the young professional cohort's preference for flexibility over ownership in the 22–32 age bracket
- Whether this preference is a cohort effect (this generation specifically) or a lifecycle effect (all generations at this age)

**Senior Living Taboo Erosion**
- Document the current state of the taboo against parents living in senior living facilities — by city, income decile, and educational level, citing any available primary research
- Document any measurable change in this taboo over the past five years using occupancy data from existing operators (Antara, Columbia Pacific, Athulya, Covai Care, Primus)
- State explicitly: if occupancy data from existing operators is unavailable, this section relies on `[SPECULATION]`

**Income Polarisation and the K-Shaped Recovery**
- The K-shaped recovery hypothesis: upper-income cohorts recovered quickly post-COVID; lower-income cohorts have not
- The real estate implication: a K-shaped recovery supports demand for premium real estate (upper band of the K) while suppressing affordable segment absorption (lower band of the K) — which segments does ENIGMA target and is it exposed to either band?
- Income growth data: RBI Household Finance and Consumption Survey, CMIE Consumer Pyramids data (if accessible)

**Dual-Income No-Kids (DINK) Urban Household**
- Prevalence and growth rate of DINK households in Tier-1 metros
- Specific real estate demand implications: larger per-capita space, amenity-premium acceptance, branded co-living and branded rental demand
- Whether Census 2011 or NFHS-5 data supports any quantification of this segment, or whether this remains `[SPECULATION]`

## Deliverable Format

A connection matrix linking each behavioural shift to one or more specific market segments from Module 03's enumerated list. Format:

| Behavioural Shift | Causal Mechanism | Market Segment(s) in Module 03 | Pace of Shift | Confidence Level | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Confidence levels must be one of: `HIGH` (supported by primary quantitative data), `MEDIUM` (supported by secondary data or operator anecdote), `LOW` (logical inference from related data), `SPECULATION` (no supporting data; reasoning chain only).

A shift categorised `SPECULATION` may be included in Module 03's assumption set only if it is explicitly flagged as such and the market projection is stress-tested against the scenario in which the shift does not occur.
